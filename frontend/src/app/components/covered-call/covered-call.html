⑤ Hedge selection — your insurance, always-on

What to buy

  • Strike: 5-8% OTM (sweet spot)
  • Expiry: 75-100 DTE (3-month protocol)
  • Size: 1 lot per CC lot
  • Hit rate target: ≥ 18% chance of finishing ITM (Δ ≥ 0.20)

When to roll

  • 🟢 DTE > 45: hold, do nothing
  • 🟡 DTE 30-45: plan a roll, scan candidates
  • 🔴 DTE ≤ 30: ROLL NOW — close + buy fresh 3-month put
The hedge math: Costs ~1.8%/yr in premium drag. In normal years you "pay" this and feel nothing. In a crash year (~once every 5-7 years), the hedge can save 5-14% of capital. Net effect over a decade: hedged portfolio ends ~5-7% AHEAD despite the drag.
⚠ Why NOT 13% OTM hedges: only ~1% chance of finishing ITM in 60 days = lottery insurance. You pay premium 99 times for one payoff that may not even cover accumulated cost. Stay in the 5-8% sweet spot.

⑧ Rolling — when, how, and the anti-whipsaw rule

How a roll works: One atomic decision = two trades.

  1. BUY back the current CC at market (or limit at midpoint)
  2. SELL a new CC at higher strike + same/next month for fresh premium

Net cash flow is usually slightly negative (~₹1-3K debit) — the new premium partially offsets the buyback cost.

The whipsaw concern

If Nifty rallies hard, Δ can keep crossing 0.40 cycle after cycle. Each roll books a small loss. But — and this matters — every rally also grows your NB position. So:

  • 3 rolls in 3 cycles = ~₹30K bleed (small)
  • Same rally = ₹4-5L paper gain on NB (much larger)
  • Net: still strongly positive, just leaving uncapped upside on the table
The 2-roll cap rule: If a single CC cycle has been rolled twice and Δ hits 0.40 a third time, STOP rolling. Let the cycle expire (take the cap loss), redeploy fresh next month at Δ 0.25 (more conservative). This stops the rolling treadmill in trending markets.
⑨ Common mistakes — what NOT to do
  • ❌ Selling CC at Δ > 0.35 for "more premium" — whip-fires the roll trigger; rolls eat the premium.
  • ❌ Skipping the hedge to save 1.8%/yr — you'll pay 13-30% in the next crash year.
  • ❌ Holding past DTE 14 — gamma + assignment risk for tiny theta. Always force-exit.
  • ❌ Panic-closing on the first dip — drawdowns recover; realising losses doesn't.
  • ❌ Selling NB to "lock gains" within year 1 — triggers 20% STCG. Wait for 12+ months for LTCG rate.
  • ❌ Trading on event days — RBI, Fed, election results, budget. Premium is rich but moves are wild.
  • ❌ Selling deep-OTM hedges (12%+) — lottery insurance, fires once in 100 cycles.
  • ❌ Choosing weekly expiries because annualised yield looks higher — paper yield ≠ realised yield once friction is counted.

📐 How the best trade is chosen — BTS scoring
BTS: NetYieldPerCycle × Safety × Liquidity × IVQuality × CycleQuality × DeltaQuality
net_yield_per_cycle_pct
(0.6 × premium − friction) / spot × 100
% of capital captured in one cycle at 60% TP, after subtracting friction (~₹3K/cycle). NOT annualised — so weeklies don't get a free "high yield" just because they cycle faster.
Safety
max(0.05, 1 − 2·|Δ|·√(days_to_50 / dte))
Reflection-principle proxy for the probability the call stays OTM during the holding window. Uses delta as the closing-ITM probability and discounts for path risk.
Liquidity
0.5 + 0.5 × min(OI / 10000, 1)
Floor 0.5, cap 1.0. Penalises thin OI where wide spreads + slippage erode the cycle's edge.
IVQuality
clip(ATM_IV / VIX, 0.8, 1.3)
Rewards selling vol when chain is RICH vs market regime. ATM_IV ≥ VIX ⇒ rich (favourable). ATM_IV < VIX ⇒ cheap (unfavourable).
CycleQuality
DTE bell curve: 1d-4d=0.40 · 5d-13d=0.70 · 14d-21d=0.95 · 22d-35d=1.00 · 36d-50d=0.85 · 51d-90d=0.65 · 90d+=0.40
Monthly (22-35 DTE) is the sweet spot at 1.00×. Weeklies penalised at 0.40× (high gamma + friction). Long-dated (>90d) penalised at 0.40× (vega bombs).
DeltaQuality
Δ ≤ 0.18=0.50 · 0.18-0.22=0.75 · 0.22-0.30=1.00 · 0.30-0.35=0.80 · 0.35-0.40=0.55 · ≥0.40=0.30
Δ 0.22-0.30 is the ideal entry band at 1.00×. Higher-delta strikes (≥ 0.35) get penalised at 0.55-0.30× because they would whip-fire the Δ 0.40 roll-up trigger on any rally — premium gets given back as roll friction.
Why monthlies now win: OLD score used 365/days_to_50 annualisation that inflated weeklies. BTS uses per-cycle yield × cycle_quality penalty. Weeklies now dampened by 0.40×, monthlies stay at 1.00×.
Why high-delta strikes now lose: Old algorithm let high-delta strikes win on premium alone. New algorithm penalises Δ ≥ 0.35 at 0.55-0.30× because they trigger the Δ 0.40 roll-up almost immediately — the rich premium gets given back as roll friction within 1-2 cycles. Rolling at near-ATM costs much more than rolling at Δ 0.40 OTM.
Tie-breaker: When BTS scores are close, prefer the strike inside Δ 0.25-0.30 (in_entry_band = true) — more breathing room before the Δ 0.40 roll-up fires.
Note on the score field: The score field below is the legacy formula kept for back-compat. The actual ranking now uses bts. If candidates seem mis-ranked vs their `score` values, that's expected — sort by BTS, not score.
